### Introduction

* Dear brothers and sisters, thank God for the opportunity to be together and listen to God's Word\!  
* Pastor Victor has asked me to share the Bible reading and listening plan that has helped me, called Professor Horner's Bible Reading System. Before explaining the plan, I want us to look at why spending time in God's Word matters.

### Renewing Our Minds: Romans 12:1-2

* If you have your Bibles, whether as a book or on your phone, I invite you to open up to Romans chapter 12\. We will be looking at the first few verses.  
* 1 Therefore I urge you, brethren, by the mercies of God, to present your bodies a living and holy sacrifice, acceptable to God, which is your spiritual service of worship. 2 And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, so that you may prove what the will of God is, that which is good and acceptable and perfect.  
* In the preceding chapters, Apostle Paul has been describing God's mercy and how we are saved through faith in Christ.  
* At the end of chapter 11, Apostle Paul exclaims: “For from Him and through Him and to Him are all things. To Him be the glory forever. Amen.”  
* Chapter 12 turns to the practical matters of Christian living. In light of God's mercy, Paul urges us to offer our lives to God and let what we believe shape how we live, day after day.  
* Be a living sacrifice, which sounds like a contradiction. We can't just get it done once and then live however we want. This is the fitting service we can offer to God\!  
* Verse 2 tells us not to conform to this world, but to be transformed by the renewing of our minds. The result is knowing God's good and acceptable and perfect will.  
* I want to focus on the second verse, specifically the phrase, “be transformed by the renewal of your mind”. This touches everyday life: how we interact with those near us, what we worry about, and what we make time for. One practical way to seek that renewal is to spend time regularly in God's Word, asking Him to help us understand it and put it into practice.

### Seeing the Whole Story

* Reading the whole Bible helps us see how each part fits into the larger story.  
* We often talk of the Good News, the Gospel, that is revealed in the Bible. What is the Good News? Put simply: Jesus Christ, who was fully God and fully man, came to Earth and lived a perfect life. He died for our sins, rose on the third day, and now reigns in heaven.  
* But why did He have to die? Why do we need to be saved? The rest of Scripture helps us understand those questions.  
* If we read the whole Bible, we will see the context. There are four major themes: creation, fall, redemption, and restoration/consummation. God created us for fellowship with Him. Sin broke that relationship. Christ came to redeem us, and God has promised to make all things new.  
* With God's help, understanding this whole story and our place in it helps us see why we need to be saved, how we are saved, and what the future holds.

### A Practical Reading Plan

* How can we make that kind of reading part of everyday life? Let me share a few practical suggestions.  
* If you have recently come to know God, or have never read or listened to the whole Bible, I encourage you to begin. The church has resources to help. You can also use a Bible app (I use YouVersion) and select a reading plan. Begin with an amount you can manage regularly.  
  * As Gregory the Great put it, “For as the word of God, by the mysteries which it contains, exercises the understanding of the wise, so usually by what presents itself on the outside, it nurses the simple-minded.  It presenteth in open day that wherewith the little ones may be fed; it keepeth in secret that whereby men of a loftier range may be held in suspense of admiration.  It is, as it were, a kind of river, if I may so liken it, which is both shallow and deep, wherein both the lamb may find a footing, and the elephant float at large.” \-- Gregory the Great, Morals on the Book of Job, Epistle to Leander, IV (1844 English translation)  
* When you feel ready for more intensive reading, I recommend Professor Grant Horner's system. You can follow it in the Bible app or use printed bookmarks. I especially recommend listening to the Bible.  
* How does it work? The system divides the Bible into ten lists. Each day, you read or listen to one chapter from each list, ten chapters in all. When you finish a list, you start it again while continuing through the others. Because the lists have different lengths, you encounter different passages alongside one another over time.  
* Over time, you become more familiar with God's Word and notice connections between the Old and New Testaments. A familiar passage may help you understand something you are reading elsewhere.

### Habits and Deeper Study

* A few practical points of advice:  
  * Be deliberate about forming good habits. Set aside time daily or regularly, and connect it to something you already do. For example, I use my Metro commute to listen to the Bible. Where could you make room for God's Word in your routine?  
  * You don't have to resolve every difficult passage immediately. Keep reading and bring your questions, but also make room for deeper study. Consider the Bible College opportunity available through the alliance. We need both: broad reading helps us see the whole picture; deeper study helps us understand particular passages. Each strengthens the other, and we begin to see how Scripture helps explain Scripture.  
  * If you miss a day, don't worry about it. Just pick up the next day, or as soon as you're able.  
  * The Bible app has a feature where it shows you a streak of how many days you listened to or read the Bible in a row. At one point, I had a streak of more than 1,000 days. After a trip overseas, I discovered I had missed a day. I was doubly annoyed: first that I had missed the day, and then that I was so emotional about a number on a screen. After all, God was not going to punish me because I hadn't listened to the Bible in the last 24 hours\! I turned off that feature, remembering why I was listening to the Bible in the first place.  
  * There will also be days when we don't feel like reading or listening. A steady habit can help us return to God's Word, even when we are tired or distracted. A missed day is a reason to begin again, and a difficult day is an opportunity to turn to Him.

### Responding to God's Word

* This brings us back to Romans 12\. As we receive God's Word, pray over it, and put it into practice, we are seeking the renewal Paul describes. We are asking God to change our thinking and the way we live.  
* You will also be able to discern God's will in the Word of God. Paul describes God's will as good and acceptable and perfect. What a blessing it is to experience different aspects of His will\!  
* Dear friend, if you don't know God personally, if you don't believe or you doubt His existence, I encourage you to read or listen to the Bible. Many people in this congregation have seen how God can use His Word to lead a person to faith, perhaps in their own lives. As you read, consider what it says about Jesus Christ, and bring your questions to God. He calls us to repent and believe in Christ. You can become a son of God\!  
* And if you've been a Christian for a little bit or for a long while, I encourage you to spend time regularly in God's Word. All of us could use a little bit of renewing of the mind. This week, choose a regular time to read or listen. Begin with an amount you can manage, and ask God to help you understand what you hear and put it into practice.  
* And so, praise God that He gave us an opportunity to be here together, that He gave us His Word, and that He encourages us to renew our minds and be transformed to know His will. Let's pray.
